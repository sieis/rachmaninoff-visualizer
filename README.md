# rachmaninoff-visualizer

A live piano visualizer for a connected MIDI instrument. One static file, no build.

**Live:** https://rachmaninoff-visualizer.netlify.app/

- Open `index.html` in Chrome or Edge (Web MIDI needs HTTPS or localhost), plug in a MIDI keyboard, play.
- `F` or double-click for fullscreen, `H` to toggle the controls.
- Notes (or `N`) opens a see-through box over the visualizer that writes what you play on a grand staff. Drag it anywhere and resize it from its corner. The gear in its corner drops down its settings, and clicking it again hides them: Show picks Bars or Chord, Names shows or hides the names, Bars sets how many fit, and Opacity sets how see-through the box is. Below those is a copy of the Click settings. Bars writes each note in time from left to right, on an eighth-note grid at the click track's tempo and meter, with a line after each note for as long as it was held. Notes struck a moment apart count as one chord. Each chord is named above the staff it sits on, and other notes by their own names, with bass names between the staves. Once the page fills, the next note clears the staff and starts again at the left. Chord shows only what you hold now. Notes are spelled to fit the chord (E♭m7 gives G♭, not F♯). Controls opacity (Windows) sets how see-through the controls box is. None of these settings, nor the Click ones, are part of a preset or Randomize.
- Start click (Click), or `M`, runs a metronome with the first beat of each bar pitched higher. Tempo, Beats per bar, and Volume set it. While it runs, the notes window's Bars start on its first click and the page turns with it, played or not. With it off, the first note you play starts the bars at the same tempo.
- A sacred geometry backdrop turns behind the notes, colored from the same hue range as the keyboard. Pick one of thirteen shapes under Geometry in the controls and set its complexity, size, line weight, spin (either direction), opacity, and saturation.
- The backdrop listens to what you play. Each layer swells and brightens with its register, the hue drifts with the key you are in, and lines stitched between your notes build a kaleidoscope figure that fades after you let go. Play response (Reaction) sets how much, and 0 turns it off.
- Set Geometry appears (Reaction) to When played and the backdrop has no existence of its own. Each note draws a few rings of the figure's own lines: its register picks the layer, bass at the rim and treble at the core, and its pitch picks which lines. They draw themselves in around the figure, shimmer while the key is held, then unravel and fade after the release. Draw speed and Fade time, shown while this mode is on, set how fast the lines draw in and how long they linger.
- Among the shapes: Circle of fifths joins the notes you hold into the shape of their chord, and Platonic solids turn in three dimensions.
- Two rows of buttons at the top of Keyboard, MIDI and Microphone, pick what a note sends up the screen. Each source has its own mode, so MIDI can play Thunder while the microphone draws Wireframe. The modes are: Off sends nothing: the keys light up in the note's color and glow, and only Glow, Hue, Color spread, and Keyboard height remain. Note visualizer is the rising bars with every option. Wireframe turns the backdrop grey and fires a wire from each key to a random point on its rim; where the wire lands, the note's color runs into the figure in threads along its own lines for as long as the key is held. Even a short note spreads far: Spread time (Reaction) sets how long the color keeps running after the wire lands, before it starts to fade. Shimmer, Electric, and Sparks add pulses of light, flickering arcs, and particles to the threads while they draw. With Held notes set to Keep spreading, a held key goes on spreading past that until it is released. Draw speed and Fade time set how fast the threads run and how long the fade takes. Thunder sends nothing up from the keys: each note lights a cloud of its color at a random place in the background, like lightning inside it, and the clouds blend where they overlap. A harder strike makes a bigger cloud. Cloud opacity, Cloud brightness, Cloud size, and Cloud fade set the look and how long a cloud lingers after the key is released.
- Start microphone (Microphone) lets the pitches it hears play the visualizer like keys, in the mode chosen under Microphone in Keyboard. Listen for picks One note, for a voice or a single instrument, or Chords, for a piano or anything playing several notes at once. Sensitivity sets how quiet a sound it will pick up. The section shows the notes it is hearing. Chords can miss a note an octave above another note in the chord.
- Dissolve (Keyboard), in Note visualizer, fades the bars out past a line above the keyboard and turns them into wisps of smoke that curl up and fade. Dissolve height sets how far up that line sits, from just above the keys to the top of the screen. Smoke amount sets how much smoke the bars give off, and Smoke spread how far it drifts and swells before it fades. Each note varies a little from these, so no two dissolve alike.
- Color by (Keyboard) picks what sets a note's color: its place on the keyboard, its pitch class so octaves match, or how hard it was struck. Velocity sensitivity sets how much a hard strike brightens and widens its bar.
- Particles have their own section: amount, speed, spread, size, and lifetime. Type picks Standard, Flame (buoyant embers that rise and cool from yellow to red), or Water (droplets thrown up that fall back to the keys).
- Strip picks what runs along the top of the keys: Felt, the thin colored line; LED, a band of lamps in each key's color that flare over played keys; Fire, a bed of flame that leaps higher over played keys; or Water, a channel whose surface rolls on its own, ripples out from each strike, and glows from below in the note's color. Brightness sets how bright the LED, fire, or water burns, and Height how tall the flames stand or how deep the water runs.
- Rain or snow can fall behind the notes and splash on the top edge of the keyboard (Weather). Wind leans it either way and Fall speed sets its pace.
- Upload a picture to sit behind everything (Image), then set its opacity, blur, and dim. It stays in your browser and is not part of a preset.
- Deploys to Netlify as is.
